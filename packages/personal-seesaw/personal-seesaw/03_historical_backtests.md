# 3. Historical Backtests

Historical backtesting asks whether Personal Seesaw would have made useful **ex ante distinctions**.

For each case imagine standing shortly before the technology becomes dominant:

1. What output is becoming cheap?
2. Is the full workflow chain closing?
3. Which human motives remain?
4. Which adjacent asset/skill rises in value?
5. What should an 18-year-old do with the next 5,000 hours?

---

## Case 1 — Power looms vs handloom weaving

Mechanized production sharply increased machine productivity and reorganized textile production. Acemoglu's historical review reports evidence that real wages for handloom weavers more than halved between 1806 and 1820.

Naive forecast: **weaving disappears.**

Better decomposition: commodity cloth and craft weaving are different goods.

For commodity cloth:
- machine supply exploded,
- source premium for mass buyers was weak,
- chain closure improved.

For weaving as craft:
- mastery,
- tradition,
- identity,
- premium provenance

could survive.

Personal Seesaw:
- **STOP** treating handloom commodity production as durable economic scarcity.
- **START/OWN** mechanized production, maintenance, distribution, design, capital.
- **PRACTICE** hand weaving as craft/provenance, not as cheapest cloth production.

Verdict: strong hit.

---

## Case 2 — Photography vs representational portrait production

Photography made literal visual capture dramatically cheaper and faster than commissioned painting.

Naive forecast: **painting is obsolete.**

Decompose:

\[
portrait=likeness+interpretation+status+relationship+style+provenance.
\]

Photography attacked likeness most directly. It did not automatically remove:
- interpretation,
- artist identity,
- prestige,
- expressive distortion,
- object value,
- ritual,
- human provenance.

Personal Seesaw:
- **STOP** assuming representational accuracy is a durable moat.
- **START/PRACTICE** forms where selection, interpretation and personal language are the product.

Verdict: strong conceptual hit.

---

## Case 3 — Human computers vs electronic computers

Organizations once employed people whose occupation was numerical calculation. Electronic computers progressively absorbed calculation.

NASA records the transition directly: former human computers moved into the Analysis and Computation Division; Dorothy Vaughan became an expert FORTRAN programmer.

Naive forecast: **mathematical people become useless.**

Better decomposition:
- arithmetic throughput became cheap;
- specifying problems,
- programming,
- numerical methods,
- verification,
- scientific interpretation

became more valuable.

Personal Seesaw:
- **STOP** accumulating calculation speed as primary moat.
- **START** programming, model specification, scientific interpretation.

Verdict: excellent hit.

---

## Case 4 — Secretarial typing/shorthand vs word processing

Office machinery reduced the cost of editing, copying, formatting and transcription.

Naive forecast: **administrative workers disappear.**

Better decomposition:
Typing speed is one task, not the full workflow. Some clerical tasks commoditized while coordination, scheduling, exceptions and organizational knowledge remained.

Personal Seesaw:
- **STOP** treating keystroke throughput as durable specialist scarcity.
- **START** software-layer competence and higher-level coordination.

Verdict: mixed-positive. Occupation labels can survive while task content changes.

---

## Case 5 — ATMs vs bank tellers

ATMs automated one of the most visible teller tasks.

Naive forecast: **tellers disappear.**

The famous teller case warns against one-step automation forecasts. Branch economics, demand expansion and task redesign mattered.

A correct model separates:
- cash dispensing: high substitution,
- customer exceptions/service/sales: lower substitution at the time,
- branch network economics: endogenous.

Lesson: model **demand elasticity and workflow redesign**, not only task exposure.

This backtest prevents Personal Seesaw from becoming simplistic automation doom.

---

## Case 6 — Chess engines vs human chess

Machines became superhuman at move selection.

Naive forecast: **chess is over.**

Chess contains at least three products:
1. objectively strongest move,
2. human competition under shared constraints,
3. spectator narrative/identity/mastery.

AI destroyed human scarcity in (1), not (2) or (3).

Human-machine centaur play also created a transitional complementary skill: deciding where human/machine strengths mattered.

Personal Seesaw:
- **STOP** monetizing "I calculate better than machines" as durable thesis.
- **PRACTICE** chess for sport/mastery/community.
- **START/OWN** teaching, events, interpretation, new formats when relevant.

Verdict: very strong support for output-vs-activity distinction.

---

## Case 7 — GPS vs navigation

Satellite navigation made accurate positioning and routing cheap and ubiquitous.

Naive forecast: **navigation skill is useless.**

Routine position-finding and route optimization became highly automated.

But:
- exception handling,
- seamanship,
- situational judgment,
- resilience to system failure,
- legal responsibility,
- local physical knowledge

remain distinct.

Personal Seesaw:
- **STOP** overinvesting in routine coordinate calculation as main value.
- **PRACTICE/retain** manual competence where failure consequences justify redundancy.
- **START** systems integration and operational judgment.

Verdict: good, with a safety caveat: resilience can preserve apparently obsolete skills.

---

## Case 8 — Desktop publishing vs manual typesetting/layout

Personal computers combined composition, editing, typography and layout into software workflows.

Naive forecast: **design disappears.**

Mechanical composition/production became cheaper; taste, hierarchy, communication and brand systems remained.

Personal Seesaw:
- **STOP** investing in mechanical production steps when software closes the chain.
- **START** the higher-level design/distribution layer.

Verdict: strong match to generative media.

---

## Case 9 — Calculators/spreadsheets vs manual arithmetic

Cheap calculators and spreadsheets removed repetitive calculation.

Naive forecast: **mathematics no longer matters.**

Arithmetic execution and mathematical modeling are distinct.

Machine arithmetic can increase the return to:
- choosing variables,
- constructing models,
- detecting nonsense,
- interpreting results.

Personal Seesaw:
- **STOP** treating calculation speed as the scarce endpoint.
- **START** abstraction/model selection.
- **PRACTICE** arithmetic where it builds number sense.

Verdict: strong.

---

## Case 10 — Recorded/synthetic media vs live human performance

Successive technologies make reproduction and now generation of music progressively cheaper.

Naive forecast: **excellent generated music makes human performance pointless.**

Listeners also consume:
- creator identity,
- ritual,
- co-presence,
- biography,
- fandom,
- difficulty/effort,
- eventness,
- participation.

Machine generation competes directly with acoustic pattern. It does not automatically replicate a shared event involving a valued person.

Personal Seesaw predicts market bifurcation:

**Utility media:** abundant, personalized, increasingly synthetic.

**Human testimony/performance:** provenance-rich, relational, event-like.

Verdict: still unfolding. Existing AI-art experiments already show source labels can affect value even with controlled artifacts.

## Case 11 — Navier–Stokes as epistemic compression

The literal claim that Navier–Stokes "signaled mathematicians had been superseded" is historically wrong. The equations became a foundational mathematical object and generated enormous further mathematics; major questions remain open.

But the intuition points at a different phenomenon:

> A formal representation can compress fragmented practical intuition into a portable object on which new reasoning layers can build.

That can devalue some local heuristic expertise while increasing the value of people operating at the new abstraction layer.

Pattern:

\[
distributed\ craft\ knowledge
\rightarrow
formal\ representation
\rightarrow
portable\ computation
\rightarrow
new\ abstractions.
\]

For AI, foundation/world models may be analogous: many transformations become callable primitives, pushing value toward problem choice, observation, objectives, responsibility and whatever remains outside the model.

# Cross-case rules

## A — Commodity execution loses first
When technology increases supply of a standardized output, rents to manual execution fall.

## B — Jobs are bundles
Automating one task can change a job instead of deleting it.

## C — The abstraction layer moves upward
Human computers → programmers. Arithmetic → modeling. Typesetting → design systems.

## D — Human constraint can become the game
Chess survives partly because humans agree not to be engines. Sport is an economy of voluntarily constrained biological agents.

## E — Provenance can make a different good
Handmade, live, signed, witnessed and biographical artifacts are not perfect substitutes for machine-identical outputs when source matters.

## F — Resilience preserves obsolete competence
Navigation, aviation and other high-consequence systems may retain manual skills because automated systems fail.

# Backtest verdict

Personal Seesaw fails if it says:

> Whenever machines get better, stop doing the human activity.

It performs much better if it says:

> Stop treating the automated component as scarce. Identify residual motives, complementary layers and ownership positions. Decide explicitly whether you are pursuing market return or human practice.
