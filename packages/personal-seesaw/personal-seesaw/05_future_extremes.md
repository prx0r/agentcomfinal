# 5. Logical Extremes

This deliberately pushes the framework to its limits.

## A — Perfect synthetic media on demand

Assume AI can create an excellent personalized novel, film, album or game in seconds at negligible cost.

Then:

\[
P(media\ execution)\rightarrow0.
\]

Scarce variables move to:
- attention,
- shared reference,
- eventness,
- human provenance,
- canon formation,
- biography,
- relationships.

Prediction:

**Synthetic media** dominates utility consumption.

**Human media** becomes more like sport, testimony, ritual, collecting, fashion and live culture.

---

## B — AI is better than every human musician

Would humans stop playing?

No strong reason to expect that, because:
- playing changes the player,
- synchronizing with others bonds groups,
- mastery is rewarding,
- performance is ritual,
- identity is involved.

Economic production and human practice separate.

Running survived motor vehicles. Chess survived engines.

---

## C — Human-made becomes a provenance class

Possible market categories:
- fully synthetic,
- human-directed synthetic,
- human-origin / AI-edited,
- human-performed,
- human-only under verified constraints,
- live witnessed creation.

A "proof of human creative process" layer could become valuable.

Danger: provenance markets can become theater, rewarding visible struggle rather than actual insight.

---

## D — Agents have enormous cognition but weak direct reality access

Agents become hungry for:
- new measurements,
- edge cases,
- local facts,
- experiments,
- physical samples,
- tacit knowledge,
- private histories,
- human reactions.

Human role shifts toward:

\[
sensor + experimenter + principal + witness + rights-holder.
\]

An agent could request:

> Resolve uncertainty X. Decision value: $27. Evidence required: measurements, photos, protocol, timestamp.

This creates a machine-demanded market for human ground truth.

Human writing for agents is valuable when it contains information not cheaply reconstructable by agents.

---

## E — AI can simulate human reactions extremely well

Ordinary focus groups and consumer testing become partly synthetic.

Residual value of real people:
- recalibration,
- distribution-shift detection,
- new cultural state,
- adversarial edge cases,
- truth about newly emerging preferences.

The stronger the simulator, the more valuable fresh out-of-distribution ground truth may become.

---

## F — AI can generate discoveries

Human curiosity does not become pointless merely because result generation is automated.

Separate:
- economic value of generating the result,
- intrinsic value of understanding/discovering.

People may study mathematics for the same reason they climb mountains after helicopters.

Professional rents can nevertheless move toward:
- goal selection,
- experiments,
- responsibility,
- governance,
- ownership,
- embodied use,
- interpretation.

---

## G — Almost all execution is cheap

Scarcity migrates toward things not copied cheaply:
- land/location,
- energy/materials,
- rights,
- scarce physical objects,
- time with particular people,
- reputation,
- trust,
- status,
- unique histories,
- attention,
- relationships,
- physical experience,
- access,
- governance/authority,
- real-world intervention,
- provenance,
- novel observations.

Seesaw at the limit:

\[
cognition\ scarcity\downarrow
\Rightarrow
complementary\ noncognitive\ constraints\uparrow.
\]

---

## H — Creativity itself becomes teachable

If the divergence curriculum works, creativity becomes partly trainable metacognition:

\[
prediction
\rightarrow observation
\rightarrow error
\rightarrow model\ revision
\rightarrow analogy
\rightarrow communication.
\]

AI can act as a lifelong mirror:
- remember predictions,
- identify blind spots,
- surface anomalies,
- suggest analogies,
- simulate audiences.

The person's asset becomes a longitudinal **world-model delta graph**.

That graph may be more valuable than any single artifact.

---

## I — Education changes

If answers and standard solutions are cheap, education should emphasize:
- good questions,
- direct observation,
- prediction before feedback,
- calibration,
- experimental design,
- taste,
- attention,
- self-knowledge,
- collaboration,
- physical competence,
- responsibility.

Reproducing known outputs trains an abundant layer.

Noticing, testing and transmitting genuinely new divergences trains closer to the frontier.

---

## J — Personal capital becomes a portfolio

A person need not demand one career provide money, identity, meaning, status and technological safety.

Hold several buckets:

### Safety assets
Health, cash, broad literacy, relationships.

### Positive-AI-beta assets
Data, distribution, authority, access, trust.

### Short-duration transition trades
Skills valuable now but with short half-lives.

### Human practices
Music, writing, philosophy, sport, craft — because they are worth living.

### Long options
Small reversible bets on asymmetric futures.

---

## Final extreme

Near-free machine cognition may force an old distinction:

> What do you do because the world pays for the output, and what do you do because doing it constitutes a good human life?

Industrial scarcity allowed those questions to blur.

AI may force them apart.
