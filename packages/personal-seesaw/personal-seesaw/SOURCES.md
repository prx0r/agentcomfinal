# Sources and Research Basis

This package mixes established findings, historical cases and new hypotheses. The latter are explicitly marked as hypotheses.

## Technology, tasks, labor and automation

- Daron Acemoglu & Pascual Restrepo, **Artificial Intelligence, Automation and Work**. NBER Working Paper 24196.  
  https://www.nber.org/papers/w24196

- Daron Acemoglu & Pascual Restrepo, **How Technology Displaces and Reinstates Labor**. NBER Working Paper 25684.  
  https://www.nber.org/papers/w25684

- Daron Acemoglu, **Learning from Ricardo and Thompson: Machinery and Labor in the Early Industrial Revolution, and in the Age of AI**. NBER Working Paper 32416.  
  https://www.nber.org/papers/w32416  
  Relevant historical result: evidence summarized in the paper indicates real wages of handloom weavers more than halved between 1806 and 1820.

- David Autor, **Applying AI to Rebuild Middle Class Jobs**. NBER Working Paper 32140.  
  https://www.nber.org/papers/w32140

- Mert Demirer, John J. Horton, Nicole Immorlica, Brendan Lucier, Peyman Shahidi, **Chaining Tasks, Redefining Work: A Theory of AI Automation** (2026).  
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15960  
  Key idea: adjacent AI-executable steps can form chains; job structure can change nonlinearly as chains become feasible.

- Benjamin G. Hyman, Benjamin Lahey, Karen Ni, Laura Pilossoph, **How Retrainable are AI-Exposed Workers?** NBER Working Paper 34174, revised 2026.  
  https://www.nber.org/papers/w34174

## AI art, source labels and emotional involvement

- Léa Bellaiche et al., **Humans versus AI: whether and why we prefer human-created compared to AI-created artwork** (2023).  
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-023-00499-6  
  Human vs AI source labels affected appraisals; narrativity, effort and AI attitudes mattered.

- Xin Zhao et al., **Less human, less art? The impact of designer identity on perceptions of art-infused product** (2025).  
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44362-025-00012-7  
  Across three studies, human-designed labels increased attitudes/purchase intention; perceived emotional involvement mediated the effect in Study 3.

- Julian van Hees et al., **Human perception of art in the age of artificial intelligence** (2025).  
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11750838/

- Brian Porter & Edouard Machery, **AI-generated poetry is indistinguishable from human-written poetry and is rated more favorably** (2024). Scientific Reports.  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-76900-1  
  Important counterweight: content-origin detection can fail. This motivates separating content quality from provenance utility.

# Epistemic status

### Better supported
- automation changes task content;
- machine and human roles can be complements and substitutes;
- mechanization can sharply reduce returns to particular crafts;
- human-vs-AI source labels can affect art valuation;
- perceived emotional involvement can mediate those preferences;
- predictive processing gives a formal vocabulary for world-model prediction error.

### Plausible, not established
- human provenance becomes more valuable as synthetic quality approaches perfection;
- agent economies create a large market for human ground truth;
- divergence journaling materially increases creativity;
- "world-model delta transmission" is a useful quantitative theory of art;
- positive-AI-beta personal capital is a superior lifetime allocation strategy.

These should be backtested.
